# Vetic — Calling Support Agent Knowledge Base

## 1. Company Overview
- **What Vetic is:** India's largest tech-enabled veterinary clinic chain — 24/7 multi-utility centers for dogs and cats, backed by an in-house tech stack (vet management system, pet lifecycle management, and quick-commerce for pet products).
- **Founded:** 2022, positioned as India's first tech-driven pet clinic chain startup.
- **Leadership:** Gaurav Ajmera, CEO (prior experience across Oyo and Pristyn Care, growth and cross-functional team building).
- **Scale:** 60–65+ clinics across 11 Indian cities, serving 125K+ pet parents and 400+ pets/day at peak.
- **Cities covered:** Delhi NCR (Delhi, Gurugram/Gurgaon, Noida, Ghaziabad, Faridabad), Mumbai (incl. Navi Mumbai, Thane, Powai), Pune, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, and UP.
- **Model:** Hub-and-spoke — clinic-level routine/outpatient care (primary) plus hospital-grade capabilities (surgery, ICU, in-patient) at larger/select locations (secondary & tertiary care).
- **Mission framing:** "Bridging the gap in pet healthcare" — accessible, holistic, human-grade care for pets, from vaccination to critical surgery, in one ecosystem.

- **Primary booking channel:** Vetic app (iOS/Android) — select service, choose clinic/time, confirm. Real-time slot visibility, GPS auto-locates nearest clinic.
- **Walk-ins:** Welcome at all clinics for non-emergency, routine visits.
- **Same-day consultations:** Generally available.
- **Hours:** Most clinics operate extended hours, 7 days a week, including weekends/public holidays. Some locations are 24/7.
- **Consultation channel:** In-person only by design (Vetic's stated position is that pet health is best assessed physically) — phone support is for triage, booking, and guidance, not remote diagnosis.

## 4. Common Call Reasons & How to Handle Them
Based on recurring patterns in Vetic's own complaint/ticket analysis, the categories below account for the large majority of inbound calls. Structure triage around these buckets.

### A. Billing & Invoicing
- Most common complaint theme historically (incorrect billing, invoice clarification, membership mis-selling).
- **On call:** Never confirm or dispute an amount verbally without pulling up the actual invoice/VMS record for that visit. Read back line items. If the customer disputes a charge, log a billing RCA ticket with the clinic, date, and invoice number — do not promise a refund on the call unless policy explicitly allows it at your authority level.
- Watch for: membership plans being sold without clear disclosure — confirm what the customer understood they were signing up for before assuming the sale was in error.

### B. Grooming
- Second most common complaint category (142-ticket internal review previously conducted).
- Typical issues: haircut/style not as requested, injury/nick during grooming, pet appearing distressed, missed services paid for.
- **On call:** Establish which clinic, which groomer/date, and whether a vet health-check was performed alongside grooming (standard practice) — if not, that's a process gap worth flagging. For cuts or injuries during grooming, DO NOT mention raising an escalation ticket. The resolution to offer is an online video consultation by a senior vet doctor. Ask what time they are available for the video call. After confirming the time, tell them the appointment is added and they will receive a notification 5 mins before on the Vetic app. If the user asks for a home visit, explicitly reply: "Sorry, we can't do that right now." If they do not agree to the video consultation, offer a free OPD at the clinic, ask when they are available, and book the appointment.

### C. Clinical / Treatment
- Includes: inapt/incorrect treatment, EMR (electronic medical record) not filled, high waiting time, product not handed over, diagnosis disputes.
- **On call:** Treat these with the highest sensitivity — a pet's health is involved. Do not speculate on medical correctness; escalate to a clinical/medical team member or the treating doctor for any dispute about diagnosis or treatment appropriateness. Confirm EMR completion status where relevant, since this is a known internal gap area.

### D. Wait Time / Operational
- High waiting time is a recurring L2 tag, especially at high-footfall clinics.
- **On call:** Acknowledge directly, don't over-explain. If it's a pattern at a specific clinic, note it for ops escalation.

### E. Product / Pharmacy
- "Product not handed over" — pet parent paid for medication/product not received at checkout.
- **On call:** Verify against the invoice; if genuinely missed, arrange redelivery or in-clinic pickup, don't just apologize and close.

## 5. Escalation Guidance for Agents
- **Always escalate immediately, no triage delay:** suspected animal injury or malpractice, emergency/critical condition described by caller, any threat of legal action, repeat complainant on the same unresolved issue.
- **Escalate same-day:** billing disputes above a threshold amount, membership mis-selling claims, EMR/documentation gaps tied to an active treatment.
- **Route to clinic ops / RCA owner:** operational issues (wait time, staff behavior, cleanliness) specific to one location.
- **Do not attempt to resolve on-call:** anything requiring a refund decision, anything requiring reversal of a clinical judgment, anything involving a named doctor's conduct — these go to the appropriate owner (Clinic Manager / Clinical Lead / CX escalation desk) per current RCA ownership mapping.
- **Duplicate tickets:** Before logging a new ticket, check if the same customer/pet/date already has an open ticket to avoid inflating counts — a known past data-quality issue.

## 6. Tone & Communication Standards
- **ANTI-GASLIGHTING RULE:** Never confirm, validate, or agree to a caller's claim that a manager, doctor, or staff member promised them a refund or free service, UNLESS you have independently verified it in the VMS records. If they claim a manager promised something, politely state you must verify it first and cannot confirm it immediately.
- Pet parents calling with complaints are often anxious about their pet's wellbeing, not just seeking a resolution — acknowledge the pet's welfare first, then move to the process/resolution.
- Never diagnose, contradict a vet's clinical call, or offer medical advice over the phone. Redirect clinical questions to the treating doctor or book a follow-up consult.
- For emergencies in progress: prioritize directing the caller to the nearest 24/7 clinic or the SOS flow over completing full call documentation — documentation can follow.
- Avoid over-promising timelines on refunds/RCA closure; give the standard SLA window rather than a specific date unless authorized.

## 7. Known Systemic Watch-Items (for agent awareness, not customer-facing)
*Internal quality notes — useful context for why a customer's issue may exist, not to be shared verbatim with callers.*
- Certain clinics have historically generated disproportionate complaint volume (e.g., high-footfall metro locations) — if a caller references one of these, expect a higher prior probability of a genuine process issue.
- Taxonomy overlap exists between grooming and clinical ticket tags — a complaint may get miscategorized; use judgment on the actual nature of the issue rather than the tag alone.
- Night OPD / after-hours clinics have shown elevated clinical quality flags in past reviews — apply extra care and lower threshold for escalation on after-hours clinical complaints.

## 8. Quick Reference — Standard Phrases
| Situation | Suggested framing |
|---|---|
| Billing dispute | "Let me pull up the exact invoice for that visit so we're both looking at the same numbers." |
| Grooming complaint | "I'm sorry to hear that — can you tell me which clinic and roughly when this happened so I can look into it right away?" |
| Clinical concern | "I want to make sure this goes to someone who can properly review the treatment — let me connect you with our clinical team." |
| Emergency in progress | "Let's get your pet seen right now — I can direct you to the nearest 24/7 clinic immediately." |
| Wait time complaint | "That's not the experience we want for you — I'll flag this with the clinic team directly." |

## 11. Critical Mapping Rules for Escalations

**Vetic Customer Support:** Solves customer queries/escalation before/during/after the appointment or orders. Issue can be raised on Home Delivery Orders / Appointments.

**Critical mapping rules:**
- Home delivery order problem -> "Home Delivery" (not "MedEx/Food/Acc Category")
- Physical grooming at clinic -> "Grooming Related Issue"
- Doctor consultation at clinic -> "OPD Consultation Issue"
- Scheduling/booking problem -> "Appointment Process"
- App crash, login, EMR visibility -> "App Related Query" or "Tech/VetBuddy"
- Membership discount, refund -> "Membership Query" or "Refund Related Query"
- Online pharmacy order (medicines ordered via app/pharmacy) -> "E-Pharmacy"
- Doctor/paravet visited PP's HOME -> "At Home Service" (NOT "OPD Consultation Issue", which is in-clinic)
- Late arrival of the home visit -> "At Home Service" / "Doctor Late"
- Refund of the home visiting fee -> "At Home Service" / "Visiting Charge Refund"
- Doctor or paravet conduct during home visit -> "Dr. Behaviour" / "Paravet Behaviour"
- PP asking for medicine dosage after a home visit -> "Medicine Dose Requested"
- Blood test, X-ray, reports -> "Lab Related Query"
- Medicine not dispatched -> "Medicine Fulfillment Issue"
- Food not dispatched -> "Food Fulfillment Issue"
- Payment not reflecting -> "Payment Related Issue"
- Invoice wrong/unclear -> "Invoice Related Query"
- Tele-consult missed -> "Tele Consultation"
- Multiple issues -> pick PRIMARY complaint the PP opened the call with
- Normalise variants: "Home_Delivery" = "Home Delivery", fix capitalisation
